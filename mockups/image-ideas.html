Thuvakkam SFS

Where photos would make the biggest difference.

Right now the whole site has exactly one image — the logo. Every design I built works without photos, using type, colour and shapes instead. But photographs of real students and real work would lift any of them far more than another round of design would. Here is what to ask for and where each shot would go, most important first.

The five that matter most

If your mentor can only get you a handful, ask for these.

1

One wide shot behind the homepage headline

Homepage · top of the page

A single strong photograph behind or beside the main headline changes the whole site in one move. Best subject: a classroom, a group of students at a Thuvakkam event, or a mentoring session — taken from slightly behind or to the side so it feels real rather than posed.

Ask for: one wide landscape photo, taken on a decent phone in daylight, at least 2000 pixels across.
2

Real faces on the student stories

Homepage · Stories page

The quote cards currently show two grey initials. A real face beside a real quote is far more convincing to a donor than any amount of design. Head-and-shoulders, outdoors or by a window.

Ask for: 3 to 6 portraits, plus written permission from the student and their parent for each one. See the privacy note below — this is the one to be careful with.
3

Photos of the actual support being given

Support page · homepage "what we do" cards

Your three cards describe fees, materials and mentoring in words. Photos of a study-kit handover, a mentoring session and a school visit would prove it instead of claiming it. These are also the easiest to get permission for, since they can be shot from behind or show hands and materials rather than faces.

Ask for: 3 photos — one per type of support. Faces not required.
4

The team or committee

About / Impact page

Donors give to people, not organisations. A photo of the committee, even an informal one from a meeting, makes Thuvakkam feel like a real group of people rather than a website. It also helps with the "is this site genuine?" problem Google flagged.

Ask for: one group photo, plus names if you want to caption it.
5

Something recognisably Tamil Nadu

Impact page · district section

A school building, a village street, a bus stop, a classroom doorway. It grounds the whole site in a real place and pairs naturally with the "8+ districts reached" figure. No people needed, so no permissions required.

Ask for: 2 or 3 location shots from districts you actually work in.

Worth having later

These add polish once the essentials are in place.

6

A photo strip along the selection process

Homepage · "how it works"

One small image per stage — a form being filled in, a document check, an interview. Turns an abstract five-step diagram into something that visibly happens.

7

Student photos on the "Our Students" page

funded-students.html

Each student card currently shows initials in a circle. A real photo would work, but this page is public and lists who is and isn't funded — so I'd only do this with clear written consent, and I'd suggest first name only.

8

Before-and-after or progress shots

Stories · Impact

A student at the start of the programme and again after two years. Powerful for donors, but only works if someone has been photographing consistently — worth starting now even if you can't use it yet.

9

Event and handover photos

Impact page gallery

Scholarship handovers, annual gatherings, workshops. A simple grid of these is one of the most convincing things a small charity can show, and it is usually the easiest material to find — check whether anyone already has a folder of event photos.

10

A photo in the donor and student portals

Donor dashboard · student portal

A small image at the top of the donor dashboard showing the student they support would make the connection feel real every time they log in. Same for a welcome banner in the student portal.

11

A proper picture for social media sharing

All pages · behind the scenes

When someone shares your link on WhatsApp or Facebook, a blank grey box appears. Adding one image tag fixes it and makes every shared link look legitimate — which matters given the "deceptive site" warning you've had. This one is quick and I can do it whenever you like.

12

Certificates and registration marks

Footer · donate page

You already have the 12A, 80G and CSR certificates. Small trust badges near the donate button — "80G tax exemption available", "Registered society since 2014" — reassure donors at the exact moment they hesitate. No photography needed.

Before using any photo of a student

These are children, most from low-income families, and the site is public. Getting this wrong would be far worse than having no photos.

  • Written permission from the student and a parent or guardian, for each photo, saying it may be used on the website and in social media.
  • First names only on public pages. Never a full name next to a photo, a school name and a district together — that is enough for a stranger to find a child.
  • Never put a photo next to financial details like family income, or next to a "rejected" status.
  • Keep a record of who consented, so you can remove a photo quickly if a family changes their mind.
  • Ask your mentor whether Thuvakkam already has a consent form. Most registered NGOs do.

If no photos are available at all

  • Do not use stock photos of students. Pictures of children who are not real Thuvakkam students, presented as if they were, would mislead donors — and you already have fake-content problems to clean up.
  • Photos of places, buildings, books, classrooms and hands need no permission and still transform a page. Start there.
  • Every design in this folder is built to work with no photos at all, so nothing is blocked. Photos would make them better, not possible.
  • A phone camera in daylight is genuinely fine. Shoot with the light behind you, hold still, and don't zoom.
